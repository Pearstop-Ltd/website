**Quick answer:** Most asset registers fail not because nobody updates them, but because the data going in was never consistent to begin with. Missing attributes, inconsistent naming, and no shared classification standard mean the register technically exists but nobody trusts it enough to use it for real decisions. AI-assisted enrichment fixes the specific gaps, it doesn't just digitise the same bad data.

## The specific gaps behind "we don't trust the register"

It's rarely one dramatic problem. It's missing install dates, inconsistent naming for the same asset type across sites, duplicate entries created by different surveys over the years, and attributes that were never captured in the first place because the original data entry didn't require them.

## What an unreliable register actually costs

A register nobody trusts gets worked around, not used. Maintenance planning reverts to tribal knowledge, capital planning relies on guesswork about remaining asset life, and every audit starts by rebuilding the same information from scratch because the register can't be relied on.

## Why AI-assisted enrichment is different from just re-surveying

Re-surveying every asset manually is slow and expensive. Enrichment uses existing data you already have, invoice history, maintenance records, nameplate data, to fill the specific gaps and standardise naming, without requiring someone to physically re-visit every asset.
